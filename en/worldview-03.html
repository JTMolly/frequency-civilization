WORLDVIEW 03

Hear every voice;
the wheel is in your hands, right now

Worry, intuition and the tiredness of the body all carry messages worth hearing; and the one who decides is the you of this moment.

Hear each voice first,
then decide which way to go now.

Each section ends with a small tag: Belief is how we see the world. It does not need to be, and cannot be, proven by science; Choice is what we have decided to do; Checkable is what can be looked at with data, and for the most part there are no results yet.

01　When it is noisy inside

There are many voices inside,
and no need to rush to follow any one of them.

A little more

You may have had times like this: several voices at once inside you. Some worry, some want to protect you, some simply do things the way they always have. There may also be one very quiet voice, reminding you of the direction you really want to go.

Sometimes the body sends a message too, telling you that you are tired, or a little tense.

The place from which we can still make our own decision, after hearing all of these voices, we call the “steering layer”.

It does not ask you to get rid of emotions, or to make any voice be quiet. Every voice can be heard; it is only that no single voice has to decide everything.

This is one way we understand ourselves, not a law that has been proven.

02　Three voices reporting in

Worry, intuition and the body
all have something to tell you.

  • The soul's signalsdirection, intuition, “this is not what I want”
  • The personality's voiceworry, habit, protection
  • The body's signalstired, tight, loose
The me of now
  1. Hear everything
  2. Judge who should step in now
  3. Decide
  4. Take responsibility
  1. Act
  2. Feedback
  3. Adjust

In our understanding, personality is a set of ways of adapting that we slowly learn in order to live on Earth. Those worries and habits are, much of the time, what the you of the past left behind to protect yourself.

The soul's signals are more like an inner sense of direction. Sometimes very quiet, just a feeling of “this is what I want”, or “this is not what I want”.

The body reminds you too. Being tired, being tense, or your shoulders rising without your noticing can all be messages worth attending to.

We try to treat these three kinds of voice as reports from different sources. All of them are worth hearing, but not every one is accurate every time, and you do not have to act on them right away.

What the steering layer does is hear them all out first, and then ask: right now, who should step in?

03　The part we can check: hear → pause → choose

Hearing a voice
does not mean you have to do what it says.

A little more

When you are afraid, you do not need to pretend you are not. Fear can ride in the car and warn you there may be danger ahead. You can hear it out, then look at the road in front of you and decide how to turn the wheel. The one holding the wheel is you.

On hearing our emotions, noticing the body, and leaving ourselves a little room to choose, there are a few studies we can look to:

  • Hearing is not pushing down: One study compared two habits for dealing with emotions. One is to push the emotion down and not show it; the other is to look at it from a different angle and understand what happened anew. It found that people who habitually look at it anew have, overall, more positive emotion, and better relationships and life satisfaction; people who habitually suppress their emotions show the opposite tendency.Gross, J. J., & John, O. P. (2003). Individual differences in two emotion regulation processes: Implications for affect, relationships, and well-being. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(2), 348–362. doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.2.348
  • Body signals also shape judgment: One experiment found that when facing an uncertain choice, bodily reactions can influence intuitive judgment. The better people could sense their own heartbeat, the stronger this influence. Sometimes it helps, and sometimes it can pull a judgment off course. So body signals are worth noticing, but you do not have to act on them directly.Dunn, B. D., Galton, H. C., Morgan, R., Evans, D., Oliver, C., Meyer, M., Cusack, R., Lawrence, A. D., & Dalgleish, T. (2010). Listening to your heart: How interoception shapes emotion experience and intuitive decision making. Psychological Science, 21(12), 1835–1844. doi.org/10.1177/0956797610389191
  • Decide beforehand what to do when stuck: A meta-analysis pooled the results of 94 studies and found that deciding in advance, “if this situation comes up, I will first do this one thing”, makes it easier to act in the moment than simply telling yourself you will do it.Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38, 69–119. doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1
  • Try giving a feeling a name: We mentioned this in Worldview 01. Research has observed that when people name their own feelings, activity in the brain regions linked to emotional reactions goes down.See the research in Worldview 01, section 03 →

The “steering layer” is our way of understanding ourselves, and a belief; what research offers is a reference on emotions, on body signals, and on preparing a pause in advance. Research supports the part that can be observed, and is not used to prove our belief.

04　When a voice is loud, who is driving

Some powers are familiar, and very useful.
But used too much, they can make the other voices hard to hear.

The 12 Root Codes are 12 powers that everyone carries. When these powers work just right, they can be your strengths; but sometimes one power is too strong, and can make you overlook other needs.

  • The power of emotional guidance (Elariah) when overused: the moment other people's emotions surge up, you sink into them too, and slowly cannot tell which feelings are your own.

    The way backMove from absorbing to letting it flow: feelings can pass through you without staying in you.

  • The power of intuitive insight (Auri'kai) when overused: you take in too much, and for a while cannot tell which parts are intuition and which are worry and unease.

    The way backMove from passively receiving to actively filtering: you get to decide when the channel is open and when it's closed.

  • The power of driving action (Rohen'dal) when overused: the moment an idea comes, you want to act on it at once, and you rush ahead before you have heard the other views.

    The way backMove from reacting to responding: check the direction first, then go full speed.

All three of these powers have something precious in them. We do not need to switch them off; we only practice leaving a little room for ourselves when the voices are loud.

Hear what it wants to tell you first, then decide which power to let carry you forward right now.

Root Codes describe tendencies in how we operate that we have observed; they are not labels used to define a person, and they may change with your state. Whether the 12 Root Codes can describe a person stably is still being validated.

05　A small practice

When you feel stuck inside,
try pausing for three seconds.

Next time something leaves your mind in a tangle, you can pause for three seconds first and gently ask yourself three questions:

  1. How is my body right now? Tight, hot, tired, or okay?
  2. Who is speaking right now? The worried you, the angry you, or the you who wants to handle this well?
  3. What do I really want this to turn into?

Ask, and then speak. The emotions can be there; the wheel is in your hands.

If you like, you can also make a promise to yourself in advance: “Next time I feel rushed inside, I will pause for three seconds first.”

You will not remember every time, and you do not have to manage it every time. Whenever you remember, just try once more.

Belief, and what can be checked

What we believe, and what we can observe,
are looked at separately.

Belief | what we believe

  • We believe every voice inside us may have a message it wants to convey. There is no need to rush to deny it, or to get rid of any voice.
  • We believe that among these voices we can slowly practice growing a place from which to choose. That place is the you of this moment. This is how we understand ourselves; it is not a scientific conclusion, and it does not need everyone to agree.

What can be checked

  • When you feel stuck, pause for three seconds first and see whether your reaction is any different: you can write it down and look back a few days later.
  • Whether your judgment changes when your body is tired or tense: this can be observed slowly in daily life.
  • Whether the pause you agreed on in advance becomes easier to do with practice: you can try it, with no need to hurry to an answer.

We do not invoke science to prove what we believe,nor do we let current scientific understanding dismiss the unknown.
We hope to be a bridge between the two:a bridge of understanding and dialogue.

These are our observations and beliefs,
not research findings.
