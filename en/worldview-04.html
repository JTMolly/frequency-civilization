WORLDVIEW 04

You don't have to break down
before you stop

Your body is not just a tool that carries you forward; it is also a partner driving alongside you. Take good care of it, and your judgment will be clearer; and growth does not have to wait for the day you can no longer hold on.

Tiredness is your body speaking;
stopping is a way forward too.

Each section ends with a small tag: Belief is how we see the world. It does not need to be, and cannot be, proven by science; Choice is what we have decided to do; Checkable is what can be looked at with data, and for the most part there are no results yet.

01　One car, two drivers

You and your body
are driving the same car together.

Many of us are used to treating the body like a car: it takes us to work, to look after others, through one task after another. When the tank is nearly empty, we want to push on a little further; when a warning light comes on, we often tell ourselves it can wait until we are less busy.

But in our understanding, the body is not just a tool whose job is to carry us forward. It is more like a partner sharing the drive, with its own signals and its own rhythm. The soul knows where it wants to go; the body reminds us how much strength we have left right now, and whether we need to rest.

We call this relationship “co-driving”: we can look at the direction together, and travel the road together. When the body sends a signal, we are willing to stop and listen, and to let it take part in the decisions that come next.

This is one way we understand ourselves, not a law that has been proven.

  • Belief
02　The body does the work, and is a partner too

We are flesh and blood.
Taking good care of it is taking responsibility for ourselves.

A little more

Ideas can travel far, but to really set out, we still need this body to go with us, step by step. When we sleep badly, eat badly, or never find time to rest, we may find the strength is not there, even when we know in our hearts what we want to do.

We hope to practice, slowly, putting care for the body back into everyday life. There is no need to wait until everything is done before we allow ourselves to sleep well and eat well; and rest does not have to be a reward we only earn after working hard.

Sleep, food and rest are part of everyday life, and they are the basics that keep both drivers alert.

The body is not an obstacle; it is the foundation. It has been with us on this road all along, and it deserves to be treated well.

03　The part we can check: when you are tired, your judgment gets tired too

Pushing on without rest
does not always get you there faster.

Sometimes we think that only by pushing past our limits and going through enough hardship have we truly made an effort, and that only then will we grow.

But we want to offer another possibility: growth does not have to wait until you break down. It can also happen while you still have something in reserve, as you slowly learn to rest, sort through your thoughts, and find a pace that suits you.

On sleep, stress and rest, there are a few studies we can look to:

  • Too little sleep can affect judgment: A review brought together research on how sleep deprivation affects cognitive function. Beyond attention and reaction speed, more complex thinking, judgment and decision-making may also be affected.Killgore, W. D. S. (2010). Effects of sleep deprivation on cognition. Progress in Brain Research, 185, 105–129. doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-444-53702-7.00007-5
  • Sleep and emotion are linked too: A research review points out that when we are short of sleep, the brain's emotional reactions to negative events may be stronger, and regulating emotions may become harder.Goldstein, A. N., & Walker, M. P. (2014). The role of sleep in emotional brain function. Annual Review of Clinical Psychology, 10, 679–708. doi.org/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-032813-153716
  • Under heavy stress, we may fall back on habit more easily: A review of animal and human research points out that stress may temporarily affect the part the prefrontal cortex plays in thinking and planning, making us more likely to follow the reactions we already know.Arnsten, A. F. T. (2009). Stress signalling pathways that impair prefrontal cortex structure and function. Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 10(6), 410–422. doi.org/10.1038/nrn2648
  • Short breaks have their value too: A meta-analysis pooling a number of studies found that short breaks of 10 minutes or less during work help raise vigor and reduce fatigue. Whether they also improve work performance depends on the type of work and the length of the break.Albulescu, P., Macsinga, I., Rusu, A., Sulea, C., Bodnaru, A., & Tulbure, B. T. (2022). "Give me a break!" A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance. PLOS ONE, 17(8), e0272460. doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0272460

“Co-driving” is our way of understanding ourselves, and a belief; what research offers is a reference on sleep, stress and rest. Research supports the part that can be observed, and is not used to prove our belief.

04　Tired but still pushing on: which power is driving?

Some powers make us very good at holding on.
But they can also let the body's voice slowly go unheard.

The 12 Root Codes are 12 powers that everyone carries. At the right moments, these powers can be our strengths; but when one of them is used too much, it can also lead us to overlook the body's needs without realizing it.

  • The power of setting the rhythm (Ori'shan) when overused: your body stiffens, expression gets blocked and emotions get stuck in your body, and you easily become anxious and worn out.

    The way backMove from controlling your body to listening to it: when the rhythm comes back, so do you.

  • The power of navigating and creating (Nael'tu) when overused: the urge to control grows and you take on too much; the pressure all piles up on you, and the more you lead, the lonelier it gets.

    The way backMove from leading to igniting: the best leadership helps everyone remember their own direction.

  • The power of steady support (Zae'rah) when overused: you suppress your feelings and avoid conflict; you look calm on the surface, but you have actually shut yourself in, your body stiffens, and relationships drift.

    The way backTell numbness apart from steadiness: being steady doesn't mean feeling nothing; it means feeling and still standing.

Every power has something precious in it. We do not need to deny it, nor rush to change ourselves.

It is only that when we notice ourselves pushing on out of habit again, we might first ask: can my body keep up right now?

Root Codes describe tendencies in how we operate that we have observed; they are not labels used to define a person, and they may change with your state. Whether the 12 Root Codes can describe a person stably is still being validated.

05　A small practice: check on the car

Before a drive, we glance at the fuel gauge;
in daily life, we can check on how we are doing, too.

Every day we plan a lot of things, but we do not always remember to ask ourselves how much strength we have today.

Try picking a set time, such as when you wake up, after lunch, or before bed, and spend 30 seconds asking yourself three questions:

  1. Roughly how much battery does my body have left right now?
  2. Have I eaten properly and drunk enough water today? Did I sleep enough last night?
  3. If the battery is below half, what is one thing I can do less of today?

Once you have asked, there is no need to rush to change anything, or to find an answer right away. Just keep practicing, slowly, bringing your attention back to yourself and listening to what your body is telling you.

If you like, you can also make a promise to yourself in advance: “When my battery drops below 30%, I will rest for ten minutes first.”

You will not remember every time, and you do not have to manage it every time. Whenever you remember, just ask once more.

We hope more people can slowly find ways of living that do not mean always pushing through.

Sleep well, eat well, and when you are tired, allow yourself a little rest. Sometimes simply hearing your body a little earlier than you did yesterday is already a very good start.

Living without wearing yourself down is a way forward too.

Belief, and what can be checked

What we believe, and what we can observe,
are looked at separately.

Belief | what we believe

  • We believe the soul and the body are partners driving together. They keep each other company and respect each other; neither is the other's tool.
  • We believe growth does not necessarily require going through pain, and does not have to wait until we break down to begin. This is how we understand ourselves; it is not a scientific conclusion, and it does not need everyone to agree.

What can be checked

  • On the days you sleep enough, are your judgment and emotions steadier? You can keep a simple record and look back after a while.
  • When you feel tired, rest for ten minutes first. When you come back, does your work feel any different? You can try it in your own life.
  • After practicing “checking on the car” for a while, do you notice sooner that you are getting tired and need rest? No need to hurry to an answer; just observe slowly.

We do not invoke science to prove what we believe,nor do we let current scientific understanding dismiss the unknown.
We hope to be a bridge between the two:a bridge of understanding and dialogue.

These are our observations and beliefs,
not research findings.
