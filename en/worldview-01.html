WORLDVIEW 01

Script and lessons:
from forgetting to remembering

Some circumstances we cannot choose; how we respond, we can.

Slow does not mean behind;
how we respond can be practiced, little by little.

Each section ends with a small tag: Belief is how we see the world. It does not need to be, and cannot be, proven by science; Choice is what we have decided to do; Checkable is what can be looked at with data, and for the most part there are no results yet.

01　From forgetting to remembering

Each of us is whole to begin with,
we just forget a little along the way.

We believe everyone is whole to begin with. Along the road of life, we sometimes forget what we were like at the start. Then, through one experience, one attempt, one moment of understanding after another, we slowly get to know ourselves again.

“Remembering” simply means coming to understand ourselves, little by little. In our worldview, this is also a stretch of road the “soul” walks.

By soul we mean the most central part of a person, the part that carries their own direction and intuition. This is one way we understand life, a belief, not an answer everyone has to accept.

Each of us gets to know ourselves at our own pace. Some people find their direction early; some need to walk a little further. Slow does not mean behind.

  • Belief
02　The script gives the lessons; the playing is yours

Lessons come,
how you play them is yours.

We use the word “script” to describe a belief: along the road of life, each of us may meet certain circumstances, and lessons we have to face again and again.

A lesson is whatever keeps coming back, giving us a chance to understand ourselves again and to think about how to face it.

This does not mean everything is already arranged and effort is meaningless. Nor does it mean that with enough effort we can change everything.

We believe there is another possibility: some circumstances we cannot choose, but how we respond shapes what this experience finally leaves behind.

But there is one thing we want to be clear about.

The “script” is for understanding the road we have walked ourselves, not for explaining other people's pain and loss. Everyone's situation carries its own weight, and we will not use this way of seeing to pass judgment on anyone else's experience.

  • Belief
03　How we respond can be practiced

Start by seeing how you feel,
and practice responding differently.

The “script” is a belief; you may believe it or not.

But in everyday life, our feelings and our responses are something we can observe, slowly.

When a familiar situation comes again, you can notice: what did you feel inside? What did you say? What did you do? And how did your body react?

There is no need to rush to change. Write it down first, and look back after a while; you may notice things you missed before.

For this loop of practice, psychology research offers some related reference points:

  • Notice and name it: Try describing what you feel in words. In experiments, when people named the emotion they saw, activity in the amygdala, a brain region involved in emotional reactions, was lower. This gives us one more way of understanding how emotions are expressed.Lieberman, M. D., Eisenberger, N. I., Crockett, M. J., Tom, S. M., Pfeifer, J. H., & Way, B. M. (2007). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. Psychological Science, 18(5), 421–428. doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x
  • Choose how to respond: Try looking at the same event from another angle; psychology calls this “cognitive reappraisal”. Research has observed that people who often regulate their emotions this way tend, on average, to have more positive emotion and better relationships. But this is an observed association, not proof of cause.Gross, J. J., & John, O. P. (2003). Individual differences in two emotion regulation processes: Implications for affect, relationships, and well-being. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(2), 348–362. doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.2.348
  • Look back and adjust: Try looking back at what you have been through as an observer would. Research suggests this kind of looking back helps people understand what happened, and makes them less likely to be overwhelmed by the emotion of the moment.Kross, E., & Ayduk, O. (2011). Making meaning out of negative experiences by self-distancing. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 20(3), 187–191. doi.org/10.1177/0963721411408883

The “script” is our belief; noticing, choosing and looking back are methods you can try. The research offers a basis for understanding those methods; it is not used to prove our belief.

Same situation, but each of us gets stuck in a different place

Even when it is the same thing, a manager pointing out your mistake in front of everyone, the reaction inside each person is a little different.

The 12 Root Codes describe exactly this: “the way you most naturally react”. When you are steady, it is a gift; when you are off balance, it becomes the place where you tend to get stuck.

  • Elariah | The Empath

    You may be swept up by the feeling of that moment, and go back to your seat still thinking about how everyone sees you.

    The way backMove from absorbing to letting it flow: feelings can pass through you without staying in you.

  • Thaleon | The Transformer

    You may keep thinking it further and further down, and fall into the drain of “it is all my fault”.

    The way backRewrite "setbacks are punishment" as "setbacks are material".

  • Rohen'dal | The Mover

    You may want to talk back on the spot, and hit back at once, carried by the emotion.

    The way backMove from reacting to responding: check the direction first, then go full speed.

Noticing, choosing and looking back: this loop is the same for everyone.
What differs is where you get stuck most easily, and which road you come back by.
What the Root Codes give you is this map that belongs to you.

You do not have to wait until things are hard to begin, and you do not have to do it well every time.

Sometimes you will notice; sometimes you will still react the familiar way. That is all right. Take your time, and keep learning from your own experience.

If one day you can understand yourself at ease, and no longer need the word “script”, or our company, that is what we mean by “graduation”.

Belief, and what can be checked

What we believe, and what we can observe,
are looked at separately.

Belief | what we believe

  • We believe everyone is whole to begin with. Life can be a journey from forgetting ourselves to slowly remembering.
  • We believe some circumstances and lessons in life meet us at different stages. We call this understanding the “script”.
  • These are our beliefs; they do not need to be, and cannot be, proved by science. You are free to see it differently.

What can be checked

  • When a similar situation comes again, what you felt and how you responded can be written down and compared after a while.
  • When you try responding another way, whether the conversations and results that follow are different is something you can observe in your own life.
  • Do these practices help people who are meeting us for the first time? Can they be done on one's own, without anyone leading? We are still checking, and are in no hurry to draw a conclusion.

We value what we believe, and we respect the way each person understands the world.
What can be observed, we observe slowly; what belongs to belief, we let remain a belief.
We do not use what can be checked to prove what we believe.

These are our observations and beliefs,
not research findings.
