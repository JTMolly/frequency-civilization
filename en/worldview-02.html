01　One sentence, and how it writes reality

Some words, said for long enough,
slowly become a part of life.

A little more

We believe a person's thoughts and spoken words do more than describe the life in front of them; they may also, little by little, shape where that life goes.

In our worldview, this is a path that runs from the inside out into life: Frequency → Root Code → Sentences → Reality. The Root Code is the soul's pronunciation, Frequency is the soul's tone of voice, and Reality is the full stop the soul writes.

This is one way we understand the world, not a law that has been proven. You may agree, or you may see it your own way.

02　A Root Code was always language

Inside every one of us,
there are a few familiar sentences.

The two words “Root Code” were first meant to describe language, quite simply. Inside each of us there may be a few very familiar sentences we keep returning to. Like the roots of a tree, they slowly grow different thoughts and responses.

Say the same thing goes badly. One person's mind first says, “I messed up again.” Another thinks, “what can I adjust this time?” The event has not changed, but starting from those two sentences, the choices that follow may be different.

03　The part we can check: state → speech → response

Our inner state
sometimes hides in the way we speak.

You may have had this experience: when you are anxious, your speech tends to become hurried; when you are calmer, you can say things clearly.

What we want to observe is a path like this: state → habitual wording and thinking → choices and responses → outcomes and relationships. These links may influence one another, but that does not mean it happens every time.

Every step on this path has research we can look to:

  • State → speech: Research points out that the words a person uses can reflect their attention, emotions and way of thinking. In everyday language we can sometimes see what a person is going through.Tausczik, Y. R., & Pennebaker, J. W. (2010). The psychological meaning of words: LIWC and computerized text analysis methods. Journal of Language and Social Psychology, 29(1), 24–54. doi.org/10.1177/0261927X09351676
  • Speech → state: How we talk to ourselves may also affect our reaction in the moment. In experiments, people who spoke to themselves using their own name, as if understanding themselves from an onlooker's point of view, were better able to stay steady under stress.Kross, E., Bruehlman-Senecal, E., Park, J., Burson, A., Dougherty, A., Shablack, H., Bremner, R., Moser, J., & Ayduk, O. (2014). Self-talk as a regulatory mechanism: How you do it matters. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 106(2), 304–324. doi.org/10.1037/a0035173
  • Wording → choice: When the same matter is described in different words, people's choices may change with it. This is the classic “framing effect” in psychology.Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1981). The framing of decisions and the psychology of choice. Science, 211(4481), 453–458. doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683
  • Putting a name to a feeling: Research has observed that when people try to put a feeling into words, activity in the brain regions linked to emotional reactions goes down. We also mentioned this in Worldview 01.See the research in Worldview 01, section 03 →

“The soul writes the full stop” is our belief; the relationship between state, language and choice is something research can speak to. Research supports the part that can be observed, and is not used to prove our belief.

We are also exploring this: when a person's state is steadier, is their speech more consistent? And the more consistent the language, is an AI's description of that person also more accurate? There are not yet enough results to draw a conclusion.

04　The same event, different words

The same event,
and we may tell ourselves different things.

When things do not go as expected, which sentence appears in your mind first?

The 12 Root Codes are 12 powers that everyone carries. Each has something precious in it, but when one power is used too much, the very sentence that was meant to protect us can make things harder and harder.

  • The power of building (Sa'karan) when overused: your mind may say, “It has to follow the plan, or something will go wrong.” You only wanted to do things well, yet without noticing, you hold on tighter and tighter.

    The way backMove from controlling to holding: let structure serve the goal, instead of making people serve the structure.

  • The power of relational resonance (Mei'hara) when overused: your mind may say, “If I do not help them, what will they do?” You care a great deal about others, yet may forget that you need care too.

    The way backMove from syncing emotions to understanding the other while keeping yourself: love isn't rescuing; it's seeing what's real together.

  • The power of steady support (Zae'rah) when overused: your mind may say, “It is fine, I am okay.” Used to holding everything up, sometimes you quietly put your own feelings away too.

    The way backTell numbness apart from steadiness: being steady doesn't mean feeling nothing; it means feeling and still standing.

You may have said all three of these sentences to yourself at one time or another.

We are not asking you to reject your original thinking, nor to force yourself to be positive. It is only that when you hear that familiar sentence, you may get a chance to find that there can be another way to respond.

Root Codes describe tendencies in how we operate; they are not labels pinned on a person, and they may change with your state. Whether the 12 Root Codes can describe a person stably is still being validated.

05　A small practice

Today, try listening
to the sentence inside you.

Find a quiet moment and think back over today. Which sentence do you say to yourself most often?

You can write it down just as it is. There is no need to rush to change it, or to judge whether the sentence is good or bad. Seeing it is already a beginning.

A few days later, come back and look again. If you like, you can also write beside it a line, “this could also be said another way,” and leave yourself a different choice.

No rush. Taking it slowly is enough.

Belief, and what can be checked

What we believe, and what we can observe,
are looked at separately.

Belief | what we believe

  • We believe every thought and every sentence may, little by little, take part in shaping our lives.
  • We believe the Root Code is the soul's pronunciation. This is the language through which we understand the world; it is not a scientific conclusion, and it does not need everyone to agree.

What can be checked

  • Whether the way you speak changes when your state is different: you can record it, then compare slowly afterwards.
  • Whether your next choices are any different after you try saying another sentence to yourself: this can be observed in your own life.
  • Whether speech is more consistent when a state is steady, and whether an AI's description is more accurate: we are still exploring, and draw no conclusion for now.

We do not invoke science to prove what we believe,nor do we let current scientific understanding dismiss the unknown.
We hope to be a bridge between the two:a bridge of understanding and dialogue.

These are our observations and beliefs,
not research findings.
